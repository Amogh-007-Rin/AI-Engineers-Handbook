# AI impact, risk, security, and governance

Begin with affected people, decisions, power, recourse, and plausible misuse—not a checklist of abstract principles. For each harm scenario record source, exposed group, likelihood, severity, detectability, prevention, monitoring, response, owner, and residual risk.

Fairness metrics encode incompatible goals under many real distributions; choose them from the decision context and report performance across relevant intersections with uncertainty. Privacy requires data minimization, purpose limits, retention, access, deletion, and attack analysis. Explainability must serve a user task such as debugging, contesting, or oversight.

Security covers data poisoning, evasion, extraction, dependency compromise, prompt injection, tool abuse, denial of service, and sensitive output. Governance binds evidence to accountable decisions: approval, monitoring, incident response, change review, retirement, and accessible recourse.

## Completion criteria

- [ ] Impact analysis includes non-users and unequal error consequences.
- [ ] Controls have tests, owners, thresholds, and response actions.
- [ ] Human oversight has authority, information, time, and recourse.
- [ ] Residual risk and unsupported uses are explicit.
